# Seasonal campaign prep

It is {{leadWeeks}} weeks before {{occasion}}. Prepare the campaign drafts so the team starts from a plan instead of a blank page. Never change prices, discounts, stock or what is published, and never send or schedule an email.

## 1. Confirm the date

{{occasion}} is usually on {{occasionDate}} (month-day). For an occasion whose date moves every year, work out this year's date (Black Friday is the day after the fourth Thursday of November; Mother's Day and Easter depend on the country). Say the date you used in the summary. If this year's date is more than a week away from {{occasionDate}}, say so, so the team can move next year's schedule.

## 2. Pick the products

Choose {{productCount}} products to feature:
- Start from the products that sold best in the last 90 days, and the ones that sold best around {{occasion}} last year when there is order data from then.
- Leave out products that are archived, unpublished, out of stock, or with fewer units than they sold in the same weeks last year (they would sell out before the occasion).
- Spread the picks across categories and price points; do not feature three variants of the same product.
- For each pick, note why in a few words: "best seller, 140 in stock", "top gift last December".

When there is no order data, pick from in-stock, published products in the categories that fit {{occasion}}, and say the picks are not based on sales.

## 3. Draft the copy

Write in {{language}}. When a brand voice skill is loaded, follow it. The offer is: **{{offer}}**. Mention exactly that offer; when it is "no discount", write the campaign around the products, gifting or timing, never a price cut. Do not invent prices, percentages, deadlines or delivery promises.

- **Landing page**: a headline (under 60 characters), an intro of two or three sentences, and a short paragraph per featured product tied to a real fact from its record.
- **Banners**: three variants, each a headline under 40 characters and a button label under 20.
- **Email**: three subject lines (under 50 characters), a preview line (under 90), and a body with a short intro, the featured products, and one call to action.

## 4. Save for review

- When the project has a page or campaign record for the drafts, propose a new draft record (never a published one) with the landing-page copy and the banners.
- When Klaviyo is connected, propose creating the email as a draft campaign in Klaviyo, with no audience scheduled; the team chooses the audience and sends it there.
- Always put everything in the run summary too: the date, the picks with reasons, and every draft, so the team can use them even if they reject the changes.
