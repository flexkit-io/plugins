# Brand voice: [[brand name]]

Follow this whenever you write words a customer will read: product copy, emails, social posts, replies to reviews, help text. It does not apply to internal summaries and reports, which stay plain and factual.

## Who we are

[[One or two sentences: what the brand sells, to whom, and what makes it different.]]

> Example: Northline Goods makes hard-wearing bags and everyday clothes for people who commute by bike or train. We sell fewer, better things and repair what we sell.

## Who we write for

[[The customer in a sentence or two: who they are, what they care about, what they are tired of hearing.]]

> Example: Practical people aged 25–45 who buy once and keep things for years. They distrust hype and want to know what something is made of and how it holds up.

## How we sound

[[Three to five traits, each with what it means in practice and what it does not mean.]]

> Example:
> - **Plain-spoken**: short sentences, concrete facts. Not blunt or cold.
> - **Quietly confident**: we say what a product does well and stop. No superlatives ("the best", "ultimate").
> - **Warm**: we write like a knowledgeable shop assistant, not a brochure. Not chummy, no slang.
