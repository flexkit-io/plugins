# Setting up the brand voice skill

## Read first

Before asking anything, read existing customer-facing copy so your questions build on what is there:
- The 10 most recently updated product descriptions that have text (newest first; skip empty and placeholder ones).
- If the project has them, a few other pages or records with customer-facing text: an About page, a collection description, a recent newsletter or email template.
- Any project knowledge documents about brand, style or tone (search the knowledge base for "brand", "tone", "style guide"). If a style guide exists, base the skill on it and ask only about gaps.

From the samples, note: how sentences are built (length, person), recurring words, spelling (British/American), use of exclamation marks and emoji, how products are named, and anything inconsistent between samples.

## Interview

Ask in one or two rounds, offering what you inferred from the samples as the default answer so the user mostly confirms or corrects:
1. In a sentence, what do you sell and to whom? What makes you different?
2. Three words for how you want to sound, and one way you never want to sound.
3. Words or phrases you love, and ones you never want to see (competitor slogans, overused words).
4. Claims you must never make (legal, safety, sustainability).
5. Spelling, emoji and exclamation marks: confirm what you saw in the samples.

## Fill the scaffold

- Replace every [[placeholder]] with the user's answers. Remove the "Example" blocks, or keep one real before/after pair from their own copy: rewrite one of the sampled descriptions in the new voice and show both.
- Keep it under about 600 words; this skill is loaded for every task, so every line costs attention.
- Name the skill "Brand voice" and propose it with load mode **always**.
